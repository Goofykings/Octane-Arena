# Rigid wheels, ball height and car-ball contacts

Wheel mounts are permanently fixed at Y = -0.12 relative to the chassis. Only
axle rotation and front steering change their transforms. The renderer no longer
queries clearance or retracts wheels individually. Wheel rays, weighted surface
normals, traction and stable/partial landing states remain in physics.

The cylinder/tread envelope now participates in the existing bounded contact
constraint. Corrections move the rigid assembly along surface normals, preserving
tangent velocity. Contact impulses use the actual cylinder support point,
including camber and tread width. Using the former virtual point with the fixed
wheel envelope initially interrupted roof recovery; the actual support point
corrects that torque arm. During established ramp contact only, detection allows
the existing maximum 4 cm correction margin. Partial landings do not acquire this
extra margin or the stable orientation controller. There are no spring forces or
new upright torques. The established ramp normal/alignment/camera controllers and
flip/recovery timing are retained.

The outer ball marker remains. A second inner ring uses a vertical ray against
the arena shell, excluding cars and goal-frame pieces. Its centre projects to
that surface, and height is ball-centre Y minus surface Y minus ball radius.
For playable height range H (ceiling minus floor minus two ball radii), let
`t = clamp(height / H, 0, 1)`:

```
inner / outer = 0.09 + 0.81 * (exp(-3*t) - exp(-3)) / (1 - exp(-3))
```

The goal interior uses its own ceiling height. This mapping emphasizes ordinary
dribble/aerial heights, stays positive and remains independent of the camera.
Local and network views use the same indicator class.

The old extra-hit routine listened only for new collision pairs, used car COM
velocity and a centre-to-centre direction, and capped the additional speed at
6 m/s. Rapier's passive response already included angular velocity, but the extra
gameplay response missed it and could miss a flick within an existing roof pair.
Flip rotation already reaches the configured 7 rad/s cap; normal aerial motion
is capped at 6. RocketSim's documented reference is 5.5 rad/s. Neither cap,
flip torque, timing, cancellation, inertia nor the current 180/42 kg car/ball
mass tuning was changed. Ball restitution, spin cap and 60 m/s speed cap remain.

Car-ball response now reads actual contact manifolds each physics step. It uses
pre-solver velocities and the real contact arm:
`v_contact = v_car + omega_car × (point - COM)`. Ball contact velocity includes
its angular contribution too. Impact strength uses true normal closing speed,
a smooth onset above 1 m/s and a speed-dependent gain inspired by RocketSim's
extra-hit curve. Grazing speed alone does not create a powerful shot. A contact
must quiet down or separate for two ticks to rearm, and a 25 ms cooldown prevents
stacking. No global ball-velocity multiplier is applied.

Resting roof contacts get a subtle tangential support impulse proportional to
the cube of each normalized local roof offset. It is zero at the centre and
increases toward edges/corners. The opposite reaction acts on the car. The actual
box collider is unchanged; poses and ball velocity are never glued or teleported.
Acceleration, braking, steering, powerslide and rotation remain genuine physical
inputs. F3 shows local contact position, velocities, classification, normal
approach, support impulse and the last extra impulse with age/count.

The repeatable tests start each dribble flick at 8 m/s with matched car/ball
velocity, use a real jump followed by a flip after 0.1 s, and record peak ball
speed. The prior impulse routine is reproduced in the same fixture for comparison:

| Flick    | Ion, previous → current (m/s) | Vector, previous → current (m/s) |
| -------- | ----------------------------: | -------------------------------: |
| Front    |                 12.56 → 16.27 |                    12.88 → 16.36 |
| Back     |                  8.93 → 11.83 |                     9.02 → 11.86 |
| Side     |                 10.09 → 12.27 |                    10.06 → 12.11 |
| Diagonal |                 12.35 → 15.98 |                    12.32 → 14.72 |

These are fixture measurements, not universal flick speeds or claims of exact
Rocket League parity. The 23 m/s front-hit fixture produces 33.73 m/s; a slow
1 m/s tap produces 1.09 m/s. Aerial and diagonal shots and zero-COM-velocity
rotating-edge impacts have separate tests. Centred and offset dribbles receive
zero artificial impact boosts over four seconds. Exact measurements live in
`ball-quality.json` and are regenerated by `tests/ball-quality.ts`.

tests, `tests/ball-quality-browser.cjs` and the existing browser smoke test.
The ramp suite retains its original assertions across 144 body/direction/input/
FPS cases. The tilted-landing, fixed-wheel clearance, goal seams, roof recovery,
ceiling release and powerslide suites also retain their existing requirements.
