# Dropshot - Hex Reactor

Dropshot is a normal gamemode in the existing Against Bots and party carousels, alongside Soccar, Heatseeker, Rumble and Hoops. Selecting it defaults to 3v3; the existing 1v1/2v2/3v3/uneven/2vBots party formats remain available. Free Play selects the fixed Dropshot arena through the existing arena picker. No separate Play Dropshot flow or new networking service is required.

## Court and floor

The original rounded hexagonal court is 45.55 m from center to each flat wall, approximately 52.60 m to the unrounded corners, and 19.86 m high. Rounded corners and 2 m floor/wall/ceiling transitions use the same generated vertices in rendering and collision.

There are exactly 140 pointy hexagonal tiles: 70 per team, arranged as 13,12,11,10,9,8,7 from the neutral divider outward (equivalently 7 through 13 toward center). Centers are 6.6 m apart; tile radius is 3.8105 m and thickness 0.55 m. The physical divider/perimeter filler remains intact; its shared material extends the same team-colored hex lattice to the trimmed arena border and center line, removing blank visual strips without changing the 140 logical tiles. These dimensions/layout are original adaptations; no proprietary assets or exact arena mesh were copied.

Tiles have three states: INTACT (0), DAMAGED (1), OPEN (2). A step from intact to damaged retains collision; a second step disables that tile's existing convex collider exactly once. Open holes have no ball collision. Cars use one continuous car-only rigid support hull at floor height, including all rigid wheel/chassis queries. Ball-only tile/filler collision and ball prediction exclude that support. Camera queries retain car floor clearance. Broken top meshes disappear. A shared instanced depth effect renders only through OPEN tile footprints. Falling tiles are pooled visual meshes, with no physics bodies.

The 140 tile colliders, neutral filler and rounded shell are static colliders rather than separate rigid bodies. Top faces share exact edges. Neutral filler triangle winding is explicitly upward: a physical center-divider drop/overtime test catches the back-facing-floor problem that raycasts alone would miss.

## Charge, floor damage and scoring

All gameplay constants live in `shared/dropshot.ts` as `DROPSHOT_CONFIG`. The [RLBot Dropshot documentation](https://wiki.rlbot.org/v5/botmaking/dropshot/) supplied behavioral reference values, adapted to this game's SI scale. The wiki gives the corner reference as 5260 uu rather than the prompt's approximate 5026 uu.

- Normal energy: below 2500; Charged: 2500 or above; Super Charged: 11000 or above.
- Energy contribution: meaningful car impact speed times 100; minimum contribution threshold 500 (5 m/s). Solver impulse divided by ball mass and sampled closing speed supply the impact measure. Continued contact does not accumulate energy every tick.
- Recent absorption capacity: 2500, decaying at 2500/second; repeated micro contacts cannot instantly supercharge.
- Minimum incoming speed along the actual collision normal: 2.5 m/s. Top contacts and side faces exposed toward an OPEN neighbor can damage. Horizontal side impacts do not need downward velocity. Side contacts do not count as floor contact for zero-second rules. Damage cooldown: 0.2 seconds.
- Precomputed deterministic graph radii 0/1/2 affect up to 1/7/19 neighboring tiles on the opposing team only. Each affected non-open tile advances one state. A valid damaging strike resets energy/recent absorption, retaining touch ownership.
- The authority latches a swept, full-mutated-ball-clearance passage through an actual open floor region. Scoring requires the ball to descend below tile thickness plus its radius plus 0.15 m. Grazing a hole or merely reaching floor level does not score. Closed-floor tunneling cannot award a goal; CCD is retained and an invalid numerical crossing is recovered safely.
- Goals use existing score, scorer ID, selected explosion, celebration and skippable replay flow. Explosion focus follows the ball below the scoring opening. Kickoff resets every tile/collider, charge, entry state, effects, replay recording and bot-visible state; match stats survive kickoffs.
- The center kickoff ball receives 10 m/s upward velocity at GO. Symmetric mode-specific spawns support one to three players per side. Countdown locking/visible steering is preserved.
- Regulation remains five minutes by default, with existing match-length overrides. At zero the airborne ball stays live until actual floor contact; tied ground contact starts overtime, and the next valid goal ends the match after replay. Cars and ceiling contact do not count as floor contact.

## Boost and statistics

Default spawn boost is 100. Held boost prevents regeneration, including when empty. After release, a 0.5 second delay precedes regeneration at 10/second. Existing boost amount mutators and Free Play's explicit infinite-boost setting retain precedence. Dropshot has neither pickup pads nor their leftover visual meshes; other modes restore their usual pads automatically.

Each tile damage step earns one Damage stat and 10 points (an original centralized score value, not a claimed exact Rocket League value). The column appears in Dropshot's live scoreboard and results only. Damage credit follows the authoritative touching player ID and event sequence, never display name. Touch/goal/assist scoring is unchanged.

Shots/saves reuse the active-shot system with sphere-swept open-floor threat prediction. Damaging closed floor alone is not a shot. A continuous open-floor trajectory earns one shot, and a meaningful defender clear can earn a save. Replay does not rerun stat awards.

## Bots

Bots use the existing generic-input controller, with a Dropshot decision module. Beginner targets the enemy half and chases with simple jumps. Intermediate values damaged/open targets, attempts raised/downward contact and defends descending threats. Advanced evaluates charged clusters, open scoring targets, danger regions and aerial intercepts. Existing dynamic first/second/third roles provide challenge, support and defensive spacing. There are no privileged car/ball forces or physics cheats. Tile state is read from the same authoritative simulation.

## Rendering, replays and networking

Floor surfaces use one instanced hex mesh, dark team colors, a shared analytic dotted/concentric hex shader, exposed-edge buffers and 48 pooled impact particles. Damaged patterns/cracks illuminate strongly. Openings expose a single shared instanced depth mesh, packed only with OPEN tile IDs. Its fragment shader clips the camera ray at floor height against each exact hex footprint; it rejects views below the floor or outside the rounded court perimeter. Repeating tinted rings and fine lines suggest depth without mirroring an entire world. No depth instances exist beneath intact or damaged tiles. Each newly opened tile flashes, falls, tilts and fades in 0.45 seconds in a fixed 64-instance visual pool; its ball collider disables immediately, independently of that animation. Replay rewind and court reset clear the pool. Eighteen poles/floodlights, six ceiling beams and a team-colored upper light strip frame the perimeter, with two unshadowed environment lights. Super charge adds a 72-particle burst, expanding wave, fast pulsing arcs/core and a brighter/thicker trail. A fixed 128-particle pool emits 150/s at Super versus 26/s at Charged, resetting on rewind/teleport/epoch change and sharing ball visibility/disposal. Normal ball skins, Heatseeker trails and selected goal effects restore when leaving the mode.

Both server and PeerJS host-worker matches run the same Dropshot authority. Clients cannot send tile-damage/charge/goal commands. Shared validated snapshots contain charge and a bounded recent event list of tile-ID/state changes. Full 140-character state reconciliation is sent once per second and on reconnection, rather than every physics tick. Client reducers restore missing deltas/full snapshots; mirror collider toggles support camera queries without advancing client physics. Stable player IDs are validated on every event. Dropshot alone permits new party members to join an active match as spectators; their session metadata reaches the existing authority without adding a player car. Reconnection/late joins force a full tile snapshot. Other modes retain their existing join rules. Static query refreshes temporarily disable all moving bodies, update the Rapier broadphase on topology changes, and restore flags without advancing car/ball positions or velocities.

Replay history stores bounded timestamped full tile/charge views at 10 Hz or state changes. Playback uses recorded state and existing ball/scorer-focused director/nametags; it never changes live damage/stats. Render-only car proxies retain the source physics configuration: the landing visual reads contactHeight, and a missing configuration previously threw during recorded landings, stopping the animation loop and skip processing. Replay snapshots now validate finite data, shape and increasing time; same-tick captures overwrite instead of appending duplicate timestamps. Invalid local clips advance to reset; invalid network clips use the existing host-authoritative skip vote. Replay clock, camera, labels and visual effects advance while live simulation is paused; tile/charge reset happens only after playback ends or is skipped. Spectator player/ball/free cameras use the same rendering and state.

Mutators retain match length, boost amount/strength, gravity, air jumps, ball speed/mass/size/bounciness. Floor clearance and prediction use the resolved ball radius/physics. Rumble is not combined with Dropshot. Soccer Training Packs retain their required Soccar geometry even after visiting Dropshot Free Play.

## Wheel contact and menu feedback

The slope correction preserves rigid wheels and existing grip/adhesion settings. Coherent, coplanar curved support uses wheel-point normal-velocity constraints rather than a center-of-mass clamp that lifted other wheels away from the curve. Three constraint iterations resolve the supported curved footprint; existing flat-floor and noncoplanar diagonal-entry handling remain intact. Weighted wheel normals blend across smooth curvature; an additional midpoint ray distinguishes a real edge, where the actual face is selected instead of inventing a diagonal plane. No springs, per-wheel visual displacement, upright snapping or additional magnetic force were introduced.

Menu feedback uses the changed control ID and one cancellable 120 ms animation per element. Unchanged value text is not rewritten, unrelated cycle values do not flash, and cosmetic selections no longer replay the entire Garage panel entrance. Hover, controller focus and layout remain intact.

## Reset contact eligibility

The polish landing ripple resembled the reset ring, but it did not award reset gameplay events. It now uses four faint wheel scuffs instead. The authoritative reset source remains actual underside BALL contact and additionally rejects partial wheel/chassis support or current solver contacts with any other surface/car. StableContact alone is not sufficient evidence of flight. Normal ground landing still refreshes normal jump eligibility without emitting a ball-reset event.
