# Bot movement, celebration pickups, and rigid roof contact

Bots keep their previous positioning and recovery policy. Boost now permits a slightly wider useful approach, shorter travel, and a smaller reserve, but stops near maximum speed. Grounded, moving, aligned bots can perform a real jump/release/forward or small diagonal dodge. Travel attempts have a three-second cooldown; close aligned ball challenges have a 2.4-second cooldown. The 0.95-second action interval gives the flip time to complete before another decision. Off-line/high ball challenges are excluded.

Both local and authoritative network matches tick boost pads during `playing` and the movable `goal` phase. Pad amounts, eligibility, pulse, effects, and respawn timers are unchanged. Countdown still freezes physics and pickups; kickoff resets the pads and car boost as before. Network pad visuals also play the existing pulse when a snapshot reports a pickup, including during celebration.

The roof ramp bug came from chassis corner safeguards clamping center-of-mass normal velocity independently of contact-point rotation. That redirected translation up the curve without supplying its corresponding contact torque. The post-step center clamp could then hold the wrong orientation. Meanwhile a supported roof had no wheel contacts, so generic aerial damping also opposed physical rotation.

Roof speculative contacts now form one support set. Four alternating solver passes apply unilateral impulses at the actual chassis corners using inverse mass and world inverse inertia. Their point velocities include angular motion; front and rear support participate instead of the front edge blocking the center alone. Penetration clearance remains bounded and positional, with normal-only point impulses where necessary. Rapier continues to handle actual volumetric chassis manifolds, friction, restitution, and dynamic collisions. No spring, roof adhesion, pitch/roll alignment, or keep-flat torque was added. Boost still changes velocity through the center of mass.

Near-touching static roof contacts suppress the generic aerial controller. Their combined normal supplies the yaw axis for weaker sliding steering, with the sign matching the inverted car's local controls. `P.car.roofYawSpeed` and `roofYawResponse` in `src/config/physics.ts` tune that steering. Normal roof friction remains 0.5; while actively steering it blends toward `roofSteeringFriction` (0.2), allowing freer rotation. Releasing steering restores the original sliding friction immediately. Unsupported aerial controls, wheel support/traction, powerslide, and the established player-requested recovery path are preserved.

`npm run test:energy-roof` exercises both bodies: flat roof coast/steer/boost, both side curves at low/medium/top speed, full roof alignment on the vertical section, front/rear clearance, containment without recovery, real bot jump/dodge/recovery, useful boost and off-line approach, both post-goal pad sizes, pulse/cooldown and kickoff reset. Also run `npm test`, the server tests, production build, and browser smoke test, which checks actual local post-goal HUD/pad feedback.
